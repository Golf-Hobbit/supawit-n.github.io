Restaurant Sales Optimization

Designing a restaurant database from scratch and using SQL to turn its orders, menu, staff, tables, and payments into practical strategies for higher revenue and customer satisfaction.

Stack: MySQL · SQL · ER Diagram · Power BI · Business Strategy

1 · Introduction

This project analyzes a restaurant’s operational data to derive actionable insights for improving business performance. A structured database covering customer orders, menu items, employee performance, table management, and payment transactions was used to uncover key trends and opportunities.

The analysis addresses four areas: revenue by menu item, customer spending behavior, employee and table efficiency, and payment method preferences. Through SQL queries and data-driven insights, it delivers strategic recommendations to optimize the menu, increase customer engagement, streamline operations, and improve payments.

ℹ️ The dataset is synthetic — designed and populated by me (Thai restaurant, Jan–May 2025) to practice end-to-end database design and analysis.

3 · Analysis

3.1 Revenue by menu items

Sales performance of each menu item — total revenue, order frequency, and category breakdown (Food, Drink, Dessert).

SELECT m.MenuName, m.Category,
       COUNT(od.OrderDetailID)                AS OrderCount,
       SUM(od.Quantity)                       AS TotalQuantity,
       SUM(od.Quantity * od.PriceAtOrderTime) AS TotalRevenue
FROM OrderDetail od
JOIN Menu m ON od.MenuID = m.MenuID
GROUP BY m.MenuID, m.MenuName, m.Category
ORDER BY TotalRevenue DESC
LIMIT 10;
  • Green Curry and Pad Thai were the top revenue items, driven by high order volume and unit price.
  • The Food category dominated revenue (~80–90%); Drinks and Desserts contributed little — an underperforming area.
  • Opportunity: combo deals (e.g. Green Curry + Thai Iced Tea) and new Drink/Dessert items.

3.2 Customer behavior

Spending patterns — average order value, items per order, and visit frequency.

SELECT c.CustomerID, c.CustomerName,
       COUNT(o.OrderID)    AS OrderCount,
       AVG(o.TotalAmount)  AS AvgOrderAmount,
       SUM(o.TotalAmount)  AS TotalSpent,
       AVG(od.Quantity)    AS AvgItemsPerOrder
FROM Customer c
LEFT JOIN OrderInfo   o  ON c.CustomerID = o.CustomerID
LEFT JOIN OrderDetail od ON o.OrderID    = od.OrderID
GROUP BY c.CustomerID, c.CustomerName
ORDER BY TotalSpent DESC
LIMIT 10;
  • Customers spend about $30–40 per order with ~1.5 items — a mid-range dining experience.
  • No repeat customers appeared in January–April — a loyalty gap.
  • Opportunity: loyalty programs and incentives for bigger orders (e.g. free dessert over $35).

3.3 Employee & table management

Order handling by employee and table usage by capacity and revenue.

SELECT e.EmployeeName, e.Position,
       COUNT(o.OrderID)   AS OrderCount,
       SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS TotalRevenue
FROM OrderInfo o
JOIN Employee e ON o.EmployeeID = e.EmployeeID
GROUP BY e.EmployeeID, e.EmployeeName, e.Position
ORDER BY OrderCount DESC, TotalRevenue DESC
LIMIT 5;
  • A few waiters handled the most orders — key people for service delivery.
  • Tables of every size (4, 8, 10, 16 seats) had similar usage (~10 orders each), but 16-seat tables had the highest average order value (~$35).
  • Opportunity: upselling training, more large tables, and group promotions.

3.4 Payment methods

Distribution of Cash, Credit Card, and Mobile Payment and their effect on order value and revenue.

SELECT p.PaymentMethod,
       COUNT(p.PaymentID) AS PaymentCount,
       AVG(o.TotalAmount) AS AvgOrderAmount,
       SUM(o.TotalAmount) AS TotalRevenue
FROM Payment p
JOIN OrderInfo o ON p.OrderID = o.OrderID
GROUP BY p.PaymentMethod
ORDER BY TotalRevenue DESC;
  • Payment methods split evenly (~33% each), with similar average order values ($30.50–31.75).
  • Credit Card ($1,062.50) and Mobile Payment ($1,047.75) slightly out-earned Cash ($1,006.50).
  • Opportunity: Mobile Payment discounts to reduce cash-handling costs.

5 · Promotion Tracking (simulation)

To practice measuring a promotion’s effect, I added promotion-tracking tables and simulated a post-promotion month (May 2025) with the Trio Deal and a 5% Mobile Payment discount, then compared it with the pre-promotion months in SQL.

SELECT DATE_FORMAT(OrderTime, '%Y-%m')  AS Month,
       COUNT(*)                         AS OrderCount,
       SUM(TotalAmount)                 AS TotalRevenue,
       ROUND(AVG(TotalAmount), 2)       AS AvgOrderAmount,
       CASE WHEN DATE_FORMAT(OrderTime, '%Y-%m') = '2025-05'
            THEN 'Post-Promotion' ELSE 'Pre-Promotion' END AS Period
FROM OrderInfo
GROUP BY DATE_FORMAT(OrderTime, '%Y-%m')
ORDER BY Month;

6 · Conclusion

The project covers the full analytics workflow on a database I designed myself: modeling the business in an ER diagram, building it in MySQL, querying it with SQL across four business areas, and translating the findings into 16 concrete, measurable strategies. The strongest levers were raising order value (combos, Trio Deal, upselling), building loyalty (no repeat customers yet), and growing Drinks & Desserts. Next steps would be tracking retention and repeat purchases and gathering menu feedback (e.g. desserts).
